Year-by-year projection

Uniform Lifetime Table (IRS Pub. 590-B, Table III)

RMD = prior Dec 31 balance ÷ distribution period for the age reached this year. Highlighted ages are the RMD years in this projection.

How this works

  • Each year's RMD is that year's starting balance (the prior Dec 31 value) divided by the Uniform Lifetime Table factor for the age reached that year.
  • Auto start age follows SECURE 2.0: born 1950 or earlier, 72; 1951–1959, 73; 1960 or later, 75. The 2024 proposed IRS regulations put 1959 births at 73.
  • Your first RMD can wait until April 1 of the following year, but then two RMDs land in the same tax year. This projection takes each RMD in the year it's due.
  • If your spouse is the sole beneficiary and more than 10 years younger, the Joint Life table applies and RMDs are smaller. Inherited accounts and Roth IRAs follow different rules. Still working? Your current employer's 401(k) may let you delay.
  • Returns are a fixed rate with no taxes or fees. Treat the output as a planning estimate, not tax advice.
  • Source: IRS Publication 590-B, Appendix B.
